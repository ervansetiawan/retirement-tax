Statutory Age Milestones
Critical statutory age milestones governing retirement contributions, early penalties, Medicare IRMAA, and mandatory RMDs.
Key statutory milestones governing retirement penalties, Social Security haircuts, Medicare IRMAA lookback, and mandatory RMDs:
Age 50 Catch-Up Contributions
Catch-up limits unlock: extra $1,000/yr for IRAs and $7,500+/yr for 401(k)/403(b) plans (higher for ages 60–63 under SECURE 2.0).
Tax Implication: Pre-tax contributions directly lower your current top marginal income tax bracket during peak earning years.
Age 55 IRS "Rule of 55"
If you separate from your employer during or after the calendar year you reach Age 55, you can take penalty-free distributions from that employer's 401(k)/403(b).
Tax Implication: Distributions are taxed as ordinary income at normal brackets, but exempt from the 10% early withdrawal penalty (IRC § 72(t)(2)(A)(v)). Does not apply to IRAs.
Age 59½ 10% Penalty Relief
Universal penalty relief threshold: the IRS 10% early withdrawal penalty fully ends across all Traditional IRAs, 401(k)s, 403(b)s, and Roth earnings.
Tax Implication: Pre-tax withdrawals taxed as regular ordinary income with zero penalties. Roth distributions are 100% tax-free (assuming the 5-year holding rule is met).
Age 62 Earliest Social Security
Earliest age to claim Social Security retirement benefits, with a permanent benefit reduction of up to 30% compared to Full Retirement Age (FRA).
Tax Implication: Triggers IRS § 86 "Tax Torpedo" sooner (up to 85% of Social Security becomes taxable if provisional income exceeds $32k MFJ / $25k Single). Also subject to annual earnings test withholding if working.
Age 65 Medicare & IRMAA Lookback
Initial Enrollment Period for Medicare Part A & Part B. High earners face IRMAA premium surcharges if MAGI exceeds statutory tiers ($206k MFJ / $103k Single).
2-Year Lookback Rule: Your Age 65 Medicare premiums are based on your tax return from Age 63! Large capital gains or Roth conversions at Ages 63–64 can trigger steep surcharges. Unlocks senior state tax deductions in many states.
Age 67 Full Retirement Age (FRA)
Full Retirement Age for anyone born 1960 or later, unlocking 100% of your earned Primary Insurance Amount (PIA).
Tax Implication: The Social Security Retirement Earnings Test permanently ends, allowing you to earn unlimited wages without any benefit clawbacks or withholding.
Age 70 Max Social Security (+24% to +32%)
Delayed retirement credits reach maximum potential (+8%/yr compounding between FRA and 70 = 124%–132% of standard FRA benefit).
Tax Implication: Maximizes guaranteed inflation-adjusted income floor. Social Security is at least 15% federally tax-free, making delayed benefits more tax-efficient than pre-tax 401(k) withdrawals. Claiming should never be delayed past Age 70.
Age 73 & 75 Mandatory RMDs (SECURE 2.0)
Forced annual Required Minimum Distributions from Traditional 401(k)s and IRAs based on IRS life expectancy tables (Age 73 if born 1951–1959; Age 75 if born 1960+).
Tax Implication: 100% ordinary income taxation with mandatory distributions that cannot be reinvested into tax-sheltered accounts. Missed RMDs trigger a 25% excise tax penalty. Strategic Roth conversions prior to RMD age permanently mitigate this forced tax.
